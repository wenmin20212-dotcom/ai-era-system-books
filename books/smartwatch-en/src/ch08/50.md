# 9.2 Asking Good Questions About an Observation: Describing the Device, Conditions, and Uncertainties

After an evening walk, you notice that the route on your watch veers into a building you never passed through. You open an AI assistant and type, “Why is my watch’s location tracking inaccurate?” It quickly lists causes such as blocked satellite signals, weather, and software faults. The answer seems comprehensive, yet it cannot explain this particular deviation. The problem is not necessarily that the AI lacks technical vocabulary, but that it still does not know which watch you wear, where you were walking, or how you noticed the unusual route.

Asking a good question does not mean replacing everyday language with technical terms. It means organizing an experience into a record that can be checked, giving the explanation a clear subject while preserving what remains uncertain.

## 9.2.1 Start by Identifying the Device

“Smartwatch” is a product category, not a device identity specific enough to determine its capabilities. Different models may use different positioning, sensing, and communication systems; different versions within the same series may also depend on different connection conditions. Before asking your question, check the watch’s settings or companion app for its model, operating system version, and the version of the relevant app. For health features, also specify the region where you use it, since feature availability may vary.

You do not need to copy every specification at once. Select information relevant to the issue. For route deviations, first establish whether the watch supports standalone satellite positioning, whether you carried a phone during recording, and which app generated the activity record. For delayed notifications, the phone’s operating system, Bluetooth connection status, and notification permissions matter more. The more irrelevant information you pile in, the easier it becomes to bury the conditions that actually affect the assessment.

If you cannot find a piece of information, simply write “not yet confirmed.” Do not turn a marketing claim such as “precise positioning” into a specific hardware configuration just to make the question look complete. Missing information is itself a clue: the AI can use it to suggest what to check next.

## 9.2.2 Replace Judgments with Observable Events

“Terrible positioning,” “erratic heart rate,” and “poor battery life” express impressions without explaining what happened. A more useful description includes the sequence of events, where they occurred, and the visible result. For example: “During the first half of the walk, the recorded track followed the road. After I passed between two tall buildings, a section of the track appeared east of the road; after I left the cluster of buildings, it returned to near the road.” This is closer to what was actually visible on the screen than “Positioning failed throughout the walk.”

You should also distinguish direct observations from your own explanations. “The track leaves the road” is an observation; “the watch antenna is broken” is an inference. “There is a gap in the heart-rate graph” is an observation; “the sensor stopped working” still requires evidence, because the gap could also relate to signal-quality filtering, recording policies, or synchronization and display. In your question, you can separate these into “What I observed” and “What I suspect caused it,” so the latter does not quietly become a premise.

Numbers help, but state where they came from. If a distance on the map is only a visual estimate, say “approximately.” If the app does not provide the raw sampling interval, do not guess a sampling rate from the number of points on the graph. Accurately stating what you do not know is more reliable than supplying seemingly precise numbers.

## 9.2.3 Add the Conditions Without Rushing to Name a Cause

The same phenomenon can arise through several different processes. An unusual route may involve satellite signal propagation, switching between positioning sources, processing of recorded data, or map display. To narrow the possibilities, describe the environment and your actions at the time: Were you on an open road or between buildings? Had you just started the activity? Did you wait for a positioning confirmation? Was the watch in power-saving mode? Did you have your phone with you?

These conditions are not there for the AI to pick a plausible-sounding story. They help compare explanations. For example, deviations concentrated near tall buildings are consistent with positioning being affected by blocked or reflected signals. That alone, however, does not establish that a particular deviation was caused by multipath effects, much less identify the algorithm used internally. A reasonable answer should explain both “why this is possible” and “what evidence is still missing.”

Simultaneous changes within a single recording are especially likely to mislead. If you happened to take a different route after updating the operating system, you cannot directly attribute differences in the track to the update. State both changes and ask the AI to explain how they complicate the comparison, rather than asking it to prove a cause you have already settled on.

## 9.2.4 Turn the Uncertainty into an Answerable Question

After describing the background, you still need to explain exactly what you want to understand. Do you want to learn the general principles, identify settings worth checking, or design a safe comparative observation? Different tasks require different evidence and answer formats. “Analyze every possible fault thoroughly” is too broad, while “Based on the available information, list two or three possible explanations and explain how to distinguish them” is more likely to produce a result you can check.

You could ask:

“I use a watch of a specified model, and I have recorded its operating system version. I carry my phone while walking, but have not yet confirmed whether the phone assists with positioning. The route shows a localized deviation between tall buildings, while other sections roughly follow the road. I would like to understand which parts of the process might be involved. Please distinguish known facts, possible explanations, and information still needed. Do not assume a specific chip or internal algorithm, and suggest checks that do not require opening the device or compromising travel safety.”

The key here is not the phrasing, but the limits placed on inference. There is no need to ask the AI to act as an expert with decades of experience or to insert terms such as filters and antenna gain. The more unverified technical details a question contains, the more likely the answer is to build on a false premise.

## 9.2.5 Follow Up with a Small Observation

After the first answer, start by adding information that is easiest to confirm and most useful for distinguishing explanations. For example, check the positioning options in the activity settings, verify whether a positioning confirmation appeared when recording began, or notice whether the same section shows another deviation during your next normal walk. Do not step into traffic or climb to a high place to reproduce an anomaly, and do not undertake extreme exercise to test the device.

Change as few conditions as possible in a comparative observation. If you want to explore the effect of nearby buildings, compare open sections with sections between buildings during your normal journey, while also noting whether you have your phone and whether the activity mode is the same. This can help reveal patterns, but it is not a product accuracy test: factors such as satellite positions and your walking route may still vary from day to day.

When following up, connect the new evidence to the original record: “There was no obvious deviation at the same location this time. Does that mean the previous explanation needs revising?” This kind of follow-up allows a hypothesis to be corrected rather than merely collecting evidence that supports it. Ultimately, a good question produces not a polished answer, but a clear path for learning: the device is identified, observations are traceable, conditions can be compared, uncertainties have defined limits, and the next step can be carried out safely.
