# 7.2 Understanding Optical Windows and Electrodes: Identifying Health-Sensing Components

Take off your watch and place it back-side up on a soft, dry cloth. You may see a dark circular window, several small windows, and metal areas arranged around them. All sit close to the skin, but they do not necessarily serve the same purpose. To identify health-sensing components, first distinguish whether you are looking at an opening for an optical path, a body-contact surface, or the electronic components hidden behind them. Appearance provides clues, but cannot establish function on its own.

## 7.2.1 Optical Windows Do Not Tell the Whole Sensor Story

A transparent or dark window on the back of a watch is primarily a protective, light-transmitting structure. It allows particular wavelengths of light to pass through while keeping sweat and dust away from the internal components. Beneath it may be light-emitting diodes or photodetectors. Some designs place several components under one window; others cover them with separate window sections.

Light-emitting diodes send light into tissue, while photodetectors receive the returning light. A photodetector converts the light it receives into an electrical signal; it is not a tiny camera photographing blood vessels. PPG estimates pulse information using components of the returning light associated with changes in blood volume. It therefore requires both types of component, along with downstream circuitry and algorithms.

Illuminated areas are usually easier to spot, but “not glowing” does not mean “not working”: infrared light is invisible to the naked eye, and receivers do not emit light themselves. Reflections on a window may also come from the cover, adhesive layers, or internal metal. A single bright spot is not enough to identify a photodetector.

Green, red, and infrared light are commonly used in different optical measurement schemes, but color is not a complete functional label. Support for blood oxygen estimation also depends on the combination of wavelengths, optical paths, acquisition circuitry, calibration models, and features enabled for the product. Seeing a red light does not establish that the device has an appropriately validated blood oxygen feature.

## 7.2.2 Looking for Optical-Path Evidence in the Layout

Why are light sources and receivers not placed arbitrarily? The emitted light must enter tissue, and some of it must then return to the receiving area. If light leaks directly from the source to the detector without following the intended path through tissue, it adds unwanted background. Designers manage these optical paths through a combination of spacing, light-blocking structures, window materials, and the shape of the watch back.

Dark partitions between windows, ring-shaped layouts, and sections of different sizes are therefore worth recording. However, they support only the inference that “optical paths may be separated here”; they do not directly reveal measurement depth or accuracy. Multiple receiving areas may help increase the useful signal, accommodate changes in contact, or provide different acquisition configurations. Their specific purpose still needs to be checked against documentation.

The identification process can be drawn as a chain of evidence:

```text
Visible rear windows
     | Visual observation
Candidate emitter and receiver locations
     | Compare official structural information or reliable teardown evidence
LED and photodetector arrangement
     | Check circuit documentation
Analog front end and digital processing chain
```

Every downward step in this diagram requires additional evidence. The first step is visual observation; only later do you gradually move into the internal structure. Do not jump directly from a photograph of the windows to a chip model, much less conclude that the health readings must be accurate.

## 7.2.3 Identifying Electrodes by Their Contact Paths

Unlike optical windows, electrodes primarily establish electrical contact. They may be on the watch back, crown, or side buttons, and may use conductive surfaces that are difficult to distinguish by color. The criterion is not whether something “looks metallic,” but whether the official instructions require the body to touch that location and what measurement path that contact establishes.

Some watches that support ECG require the wearer to touch a designated electrode with a finger of the other hand. An electrode on the wearing side contacts the wrist, while another contacts the finger, allowing the device to record the difference in electrical potential at the body surface between the two contact locations. Follow the instructions for the specific model regarding contact points and operation; do not assume they apply to every watch.

BIA also requires electrodes, but uses a different measurement mechanism: the device applies a controlled, weak alternating electrical signal to the body, measures the associated response, and uses a model to estimate body composition. It does not weigh fat directly, nor do two visible pieces of metal establish support for it. If the manual lists restrictions concerning implanted electronic devices or other conditions, observe them. Do not start a test merely to inspect the components.

Charging contacts may also be metal, but that does not make them health-measurement electrodes. Decorative rings, fastening screws, and the case do not necessarily participate in measurement either. When identifying components, record “conductive material,” “possible contact location,” and “confirmed measurement electrode” separately.

## 7.2.4 Behind the Windows Is an Analog Front End

The signal output by a photodetector is usually very weak and cannot become an on-screen heart-rate number without processing. The analog front end, or AFE, handles some of the acquisition tasks in the signal chain, such as amplification, ambient-light handling, and analog-to-digital conversion. Some devices also integrate LED drivers. The AFE works with the light sources and receivers, but is not necessarily right beside the window or visible from the back of the watch.

TI’s AFE4404 offers a publicly documented example for understanding this type of circuit. Its official product page lets you relate the LED drivers, receive channels, and digital output to one another. However, this establishes only that this particular component has those capabilities—not that your watch uses it.

Nor can a small black package in a teardown photograph be identified as an AFE by size alone. Power-management chips, memory, and other interface components may look similar. Identification requires cross-checking clear markings, package dimensions, connections, and official documentation. Even after confirming the AFE model, overall device performance still depends on optical design, wearing conditions, and algorithms. Chip specifications alone cannot determine which device performs best.

## 7.2.5 Completing an Identification Without Starting a Measurement

In normal indoor lighting, first clean and dry the watch back according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, then take an overall photograph with your phone. There is no need to force the light sources to turn on, stare into illuminated windows, or scratch a surface to test whether it conducts electricity. Keep the original photograph and mark the windows, suspected electrodes, and charging contacts on a copy.

Next, consult the manual for the specific model, looking for sensor descriptions, ECG contact diagrams, or BIA operating illustrations. Write one sentence of evidence for each mark, such as “a dark window is visible,” “official instructions identify this as an electrode,” or “the internal receiver location has not yet been confirmed.” If you share the photographs, conceal serial numbers and other device identifiers.

The final result is not a photograph crowded with guessed model numbers, but a component map that distinguishes facts, inferences, and unknowns. It connects visible surfaces to the hidden measurement chain and reminds us that recognizing a component is only the starting point for understanding a health feature—not validation of every reading it produces.
